💽 Hard drive (internal HDD) · 🗑️ Deleted files

Recover Deleted Files From an Internal Hard Drive

When you delete a file, the computer usually does not wipe it. It just marks the space as free to reuse. Your file stays on the disk until something new is saved over it, so the sooner you stop using the drive, the better your chances.

1/5 · Safe to DIY
Success odds: High

If the drive is healthy, recovery only reads data and changes nothing, as long as you save the results to a different drive.

✋ Stop! Don't do this first

  • Stop saving, downloading, installing or browsing on this drive right now. Anything new can overwrite your deleted files.
  • Do not install recovery software onto this drive. Install it on a different drive, or run it from a USB stick.
  • Do not save recovered files back onto this drive. Always choose a different drive as the destination.

Your steps

0 of 7 done
  1. Show me how
    1. If the drive is your main Windows or Mac drive: save nothing, close your programs, then shut the computer down. Windows: Start menu, Power, Shut down. Mac: Apple menu, Shut Down.
    2. If it is a second internal drive, just stop saving or downloading to it until the recovery is done.
  2. Show me how
    1. Windows: double-click Recycle Bin on the desktop, right-click your file, choose Restore. Mac: click Trash in the Dock, right-click the file, choose Put Back.
    2. Windows: also try right-click the folder, Properties, Previous Versions (this uses File History). Mac: open Time Machine from the menu bar and browse back in time.
    3. Check any cloud backup you use, such as OneDrive, iCloud or Google Drive, for a Deleted or Recently deleted section.
  3. Show me how
    1. Get an external drive or USB stick with at least as much free space as the files you want back.
    2. Plug it in and check it opens normally. Windows: File Explorer, This PC. Mac: Finder sidebar.
  4. Show me how
    1. Simplest: shut down, remove the drive, and attach it to a second computer as an extra drive (a USB adapter or dock works).
    2. Or boot from a live USB (a USB stick that starts its own small copy of Linux so nothing is installed). Our guide shows how to make one.
    3. On the Linux desktop, open a terminal (a window where you type commands) from the menu.

    Guide: How to make a Linux live USB (step by step)Guide: Data recovery glossary for beginners

  5. Show me how
    1. In the terminal, type sudo testdisk and press Enter.
    2. Choose Create (new log), pick your drive with the arrow keys, press Enter, pick the partition type it suggests (usually Intel or EFI GPT), then choose Advanced.
    3. Select the partition holding your files, choose Undelete, highlight the file, press c to copy, and pick the folder on your second drive.

    Guide: How to use TestDisk to copy files off a drive

  6. Show me how
    1. In the terminal, type sudo photorec and press Enter.
    2. Pick the drive, then the partition, then choose File Opt to tick only the file types you want, which speeds things up.
    3. Choose the folder on your second drive as the destination and press c to start. Wait until it says it has finished.

  7. Show me how
    1. Open a handful of files of each type, such as a photo, a document and a video, and make sure they open fully.
    2. Keep the original drive untouched until you are happy with the results.
    3. If you are unsure whether the drive itself is healthy, see our health check guide.

Questions people ask

Can I get back files I emptied from the Recycle Bin?

Often yes. Emptying the bin only marks the space as free, so stop using the drive right away. Some newer drives (called SMR drives) tidy up freed space on their own, which lowers the odds.

Why did my recovered files lose their names?

PhotoRec finds files by what they contain, not by the drive's records, so names and folders are not saved. TestDisk can sometimes keep names if those records are still intact.

Does it matter that I kept using the computer after deleting?

It lowers the odds a little, because new data may have landed on the freed space. Stop now and recover what is left.
