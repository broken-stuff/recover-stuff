💽 Hard drive (internal HDD) · 🔍 Not detected / not showing up

Hard Drive Not Showing Up or Not Detected

A drive that does not show up may have a loose cable, a bad port, a lost partition (the section that holds your files), or a real hardware fault. The first checks are simple and safe. If the drive is silent, clicking or very hot, the cause may be physical.

3/5 · Take care
Success odds: Varies

Cable and software checks are safe, but powering a failing drive on and off again and again can make the damage worse.

✋ Stop! Don't do this first

  • Do not keep switching the drive on and off to see if it comes back. Each try adds wear if it is failing.
  • If your computer offers to Initialize or Format the drive, click Cancel. Either one can erase your data.
  • Do not open the drive. The inside needs a cleanroom, and ordinary dust ruins it.

Your steps

0 of 7 done
  1. Show me how
    1. With the drive connected and the computer on, listen closely and rest a fingertip lightly on the drive.
    2. Soft hum or gentle vibration: it is spinning, so carry on to the next step.
    3. Clicking, grinding or beeping: unplug it and read our clicking guide. Burning smell or silence: unplug it and read our no-power guide.
  2. Show me how
    1. Restart the computer and tap the BIOS key as it starts. Common keys are Del, F2, F10 or F12; the startup screen often shows which one.
    2. Look in the Storage, Boot or SATA information section for your drive's model name and size.
    3. Listed correctly: the problem is probably software or the partition. Not listed: go to the next step. Leave the BIOS with Esc without changing anything, and choose No or Discard if it asks whether to save changes.
  3. Show me how
    1. Shut down and unplug the power. On a desktop, open the computer's side panel (never the drive itself).
    2. Swap the thin SATA data cable for another one and plug into a different SATA port on the motherboard. Press each connector in firmly.
    3. Check the wider power connector is firmly seated, or try a different one from the power supply. Then start the computer and look in the BIOS again.
  4. Show me how
    1. Windows: right-click the Start button, choose Disk Management. If a window pops up asking to initialize the disk, click Cancel. Mac: open Finder, Applications, Utilities, Disk Utility, then View, Show All Devices.
    2. Find your drive by its size. If it says Raw, Unallocated or Not Initialized, that is fine. Do not click Initialize, Format or Erase.
    3. If it is listed, note what it says and move on to the health check.
  5. Show me how
    1. Windows: install CrystalDiskInfo and open it. Look at the big Health Status box: Good is fine, Caution or Bad means it is failing.
    2. Linux live USB: press Ctrl+Alt+T, type sudo apt install smartmontools, then sudo smartctl -a /dev/sdX (replace sdX with your drive, which lsblk shows). Mac: see the health check guide.
    3. Look for Reallocated, Pending or Uncorrectable sector counts above zero. Those mean the surface is wearing out.

    Guide: How to check if a drive is healthy (SMART)Guide: Data recovery glossary for beginners

  6. Show me how
    1. Get a different drive at least as big as the failing one.
    2. Follow our imaging guide to copy the drive with ddrescue. Always type the failing drive first and the empty destination second. If you swap them, the copy erases your data.
    3. Then run TestDisk or PhotoRec on the image file, not on the original drive.

    Guide: How to make an image (exact copy) of a driveGuide: How to use TestDisk to copy files off a driveGuide: How to use PhotoRec to recover photos and files

  7. Show me how
    1. Unplug the drive and put it somewhere safe.
    2. Do not try a replacement circuit board yourself. Each board holds a small ROM chip with data unique to that drive, and it must be moved onto the new board by a specialist.
    3. Contact a professional recovery lab and describe what you tried.

Questions people ask

My drive shows up but says Raw or needs formatting. What now?

Do not format it. The drive's file records are probably damaged, and TestDisk or PhotoRec can often still reach your files, ideally working on an image (a full copy of the drive).

Could it just be a cable?

Yes, that is very common. Try another cable, another port and another power connector before assuming the drive is dead.

Can I fix it by swapping the circuit board?

Not as a DIY fix. Each board has a ROM chip with data unique to that drive, which must be moved onto the new board, so leave it to specialists.
