💽 Hard drive (internal HDD) · 🔐 Encrypted or locked

Recover Data From an Encrypted Hard Drive

Encryption scrambles your data so that only the right password or recovery key can unlock it. If the drive is healthy and you have the key, getting files back is usually simple. Without it, recovery is not realistic, and anyone promising otherwise should not be trusted.

3/5 · Take care
Success odds: Varies

Unlocking with a known key is low risk, but guessing or tampering with a failing encrypted drive can lock you out for good.

✋ Stop! Don't do this first

  • Do not format, reset or reinstall the operating system. That destroys the encryption headers and your chance of unlocking.
  • Do not keep guessing passwords on a drive that locks you out after failed tries.
  • Do not trust tools or sites that claim to crack or brute-force strong encryption.

Your steps

0 of 6 done
  1. Show me how
    1. Windows: open File Explorer. A padlock on the drive icon means BitLocker. Mac: a locked drive asks for a password when you open it in Finder, which means FileVault or Mac encryption.
    2. If you are asked for a VeraCrypt password, it is VeraCrypt. On Linux, a prompt for a passphrase at boot usually means LUKS.
  2. Show me how
    1. BitLocker: go to aka.ms/myrecoverykey (it opens account.microsoft.com/devices/recoverykey) on any device and sign in with the Microsoft account you used on the computer. Also check a work or school account.
    2. FileVault: on a Mac, use Apple Account reset on the login screen, or the recovery key you saved.
    3. Also look for a printed page or a text file on a USB stick.
  3. Show me how
    1. Search your email for BitLocker or recovery key.
    2. Check your password manager, printed papers, cloud storage and old USB sticks.
    3. A BitLocker key is a 48-digit number in groups of six digits.
  4. Show me how
    1. Windows: open the drive in File Explorer, click More options, Enter recovery key, and type it in. Mac: enter the password when asked.
    2. Copy your files to a different drive, then open a few to check them.
  5. Show me how
    1. Follow our imaging guide with ddrescue. Always type the failing drive first and the empty destination second. If you swap them, the copy erases your data.
    2. Then unlock a copy of the image with the same key and copy files out. On Ubuntu: open the Disks app, choose Attach Disk Image from its menu, pick the image, then open it in Files and type the password or key. If this is beyond you, a professional can do it.

    Guide: How to make an image (exact copy) of a drive

  6. Show me how
    1. If you have no password and no key, the data cannot realistically be recovered.
    2. If the drive is damaged and you do hold the key, a professional can image it for you.

Questions people ask

Where is my BitLocker recovery key?

Often in your Microsoft account online, or in a work or school account, a printed page, or a saved file or USB stick.

Can someone crack the encryption for me?

Not realistically. Modern encryption with a strong password is built to resist this, so be careful of anyone who says otherwise.

What if I forgot my FileVault password?

Try your Apple Account reset, or the recovery key you saved when you turned it on. Without those, the data cannot be unlocked.
