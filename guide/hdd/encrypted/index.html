💽 Hard drive (internal HDD) · 🔐 Encrypted or locked

Recover Data From an Encrypted Hard Drive

Encryption scrambles your data so that only the right password or recovery key can unlock it. If the drive is healthy and you have the key, getting files back is usually simple. Without it, recovery is not realistic, and anyone promising otherwise should not be trusted.

2/5 · Low risk
Success odds: Varies

Unlocking with a known key is low risk, but guessing or tampering with a failing encrypted drive can lock you out for good.

✋ Stop! Don't do this first

  • Do not format, reset, or reinstall the operating system. That destroys the encryption headers and your chance of unlocking.
  • Do not keep guessing passwords on a drive that locks you out after failed tries.
  • Do not trust tools or sites that claim to crack or brute-force strong encryption.

🧑‍🔧 When to call a pro

If the drive is also failing or damaged and you hold the key, a professional can image it for you. Without the key, no one can realistically recover the data.

Questions people ask

Where is my BitLocker recovery key?

Often in your Microsoft account online, or in a work or school account, a printed page, or a saved file or USB stick.

Can someone crack the encryption for me?

Not realistically. Modern encryption with a strong password is built to resist this, so be careful of anyone who says otherwise.

What if I forgot my FileVault password?

Try your Apple Account reset or the recovery key you saved when you turned it on. Without those, the data cannot be unlocked.
