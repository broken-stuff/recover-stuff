⚡ SSD (SATA and NVMe) · 🔥 Water, fire or physical damage

SSD Damaged by Water, Fire or Impact

Liquid, smoke, heat or a hard knock can short out an SSD or crack its memory chips. Turning it on can finish the damage. Your best chance is to keep it off and let a specialist look at it.

5/5 · Pro territory
Success odds: Low

Powering on or heat-drying a damaged SSD can short the board and destroy data that was otherwise safe.

✋ Stop! Don't do this first

  • Do not plug the SSD in or turn on the device it is in.
  • Do not rinse it or dry it with a hair dryer, oven, rice or any heat.
  • Do not shake it, clean the contacts or open it.

Your steps

0 of 5 done
  1. Show me how
    1. Turn the device off. If it is already off, leave it off.
    2. Unplug the charger.
    3. Remove the battery and the SSD only if they come out easily without tools. Do not force anything.
    4. If the battery is swollen, hot or smoking, do not touch it. Move away and call the fire service.
  2. Show me how
    1. Pick the SSD up by the edges. Do not touch the gold contacts.
    2. Put it in a clean, dry, anti-static bag (the shiny bag electronics come in) or a small box. A zip bag with no moisture is also fine.
    3. Do not rinse it, wipe it or dry it, whatever the liquid was.
    4. Store it somewhere cool and safe.
  3. Show me how
    1. Write down the liquid (fresh water, salt water, coffee, sugary drink, smoke) or heat source.
    2. Note how long it was wet or hot, and whether it was powered on at the time.
    3. Note the drive's make, model and size from its label.
    4. Keep the note with the drive.
  4. Show me how
    1. Check cloud services such as OneDrive, iCloud and Google Drive on their websites.
    2. Windows: look at File History or Backup. Mac: look for Time Machine drives.
    3. Check old drives, phones and other computers for copies.
    4. Check email attachments and messaging apps where you may have sent files.
  5. Show me how
    1. Search for recovery labs that say they handle liquid or fire damage.
    2. Ask about a free evaluation and 'no data, no fee' terms before you pay.
    3. Tell them exactly what you wrote down in the earlier step.
    4. Pack the SSD in an anti-static bag and bubble wrap, then ship as they instruct.

Questions people ask

Can I dry out a wet SSD and try it?

No. Even when it looks dry, leftover residue on the board can cause shorts. Do not use heat or rice.

Is a burned SSD always unrecoverable?

Not always. If the memory chips survived, a specialist may repair the board so the chips can be read, but results vary a lot.
